What is an ATS Resume Checker?

An Applicant Tracking System (ATS) is software used by over 95% of Fortune 500 companies to automatically screen resumes before a human recruiter ever sees them. When you apply for a job online, your resume almost certainly passes through an ATS first — and most are rejected at this stage.

How ATS Screening Works

1 Parse

The ATS extracts text from your resume and tries to identify your name, contact info, work history, education, and skills. If it can't read your file, you're automatically rejected.

2 Score

Your resume is scored based on keyword matches, section structure, job title relevance, and formatting. Resumes below a threshold score are filtered out before any human review.

3 Rank

Surviving resumes are ranked and surfaced to recruiters. Only the top candidates make it to human eyes — often fewer than 25% of all applicants.

What This Checker Evaluates

Parseability (30% of score)

Can an ATS actually read your file? Checks for text extraction quality, contact info presence, section headers, and overall document length.

Structure (30% of score)

Are the right sections present and complete? Checks for Experience, Education, and Skills sections, job title/company/date completeness, and bullet point coverage.

Content Quality (40% of score)

Does your writing show impact? Checks for quantified achievements (numbers, percentages), strong action verbs, appropriate bullet length, and absence of clichés and first-person pronouns.

Common ATS Pitfalls to Avoid

  • Submitting image-based or scanned PDFs — ATS cannot read them
  • Using tables, text boxes, or columns — these break ATS parsing
  • Non-standard section headers like "Where I've Worked" instead of "Experience"
  • Describing duties ("responsible for") instead of achievements ("reduced X by Y%")
  • Missing or generic skills section — ATS relies on keyword matching
